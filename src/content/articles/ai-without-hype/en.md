Artificial intelligence is simultaneously overhyped and underestimated. Some people see it as a solution to almost every problem; others mainly see risk. A more useful approach is practical: check what AI is actually good at, where it fails, and how it can fit into real work.

## Why AI seems to be everywhere

Modern language models make many tasks accessible through conversation: summarization, translation, code generation, document analysis, and information organization.

## What an LLM actually does

A language model predicts the next tokens from context. That does not reduce it to simple guessing. Scale, training, and learned parameters let it capture many patterns in language and data.

## Why opinions differ so much

The usefulness of AI depends on the task. The same model may be excellent for drafting text and unreliable for a task requiring perfect factual accuracy.

## Where AI is useful today

Common examples include working with text, programming, document analysis, prototyping, and learning.

## Where problems appear

Models can hallucinate, oversimplify, miss context, or sound confident without enough evidence. Outputs should therefore be verified in proportion to the risk.

## AI as a collaborator

A practical workflow is:

```text
human defines the goal
        ↓
AI proposes or performs part of the work
        ↓
human evaluates
        ↓
the result is refined and verified
```

## Why learn this

AI changes how we work with information and software. Understanding both capability and limitation makes it possible to use it deliberately rather than through hype or fear.
